This contract covers the two first-party surfaces the source actually inspected on 2026-06-26: the corporate homepage at `https://www.greencar.co.kr` and the G car product site at `https://www.greencar-gcar.co.kr`. Every value below stays attached to the surface that established it. The sibling live inspect records the G car document title as `롯데렌터카 G car`; that title is a sibling observation on the product surface, not a replacement for the corporate homepage title `그린카(Greencar) 공식사이트`. The boundary that stops a corporate-homepage measurement from standing in for an uninspected reservation or saved-list screen the source names in its state contract, the rule that every value stays attached to the surface that established it, and the reading of the G car title as a sibling observation rather than a replacement for the corporate title, are a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; they are not Greencar-authored or a separately published UI specification.

The captured interface layer is a pure-white canvas (`#ffffff`) broken by full-bleed bands of the signature brand green (`#00c88c` and the brighter `#00dc9a`). Body and heading text sit in pure black (`#000000`) and a softer dark chrome ink (`#222222`). The source's own token note records that green is the identity color and dark is the action color: green fills hero panels and tints accent links; action buttons are dark solid (`#222222`) plus outline. Reading the official site as a calm, editorial corporate-mobility surface rather than a transactional booking app, and reading green as the brand's "who we are" color so the eye treats green as identity and dark as action, is a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; it is not Greencar-authored or a separately published UI specification.

Latin display runs in **Outfit** — the source calls it a geometric, rounded-terminal sans — at 64px / weight 500 in deep teal-navy (`#133b55`) for the English hero tagline `Create a Better Life`. Korean headlines and body run in **Pretendard** at 16px / weight 400. Section headlines step up to 36px / weight 700 in `#222222`. The wordmark H1 `그린카` sits at 32px / 700. Calling Pretendard the de-facto Korean product font, reading Outfit as giving the tagline an open, optimistic feel, and reading the Outfit-for-latin / Pretendard-for-hangul split as the core tension of the system, is a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; it is not Greencar-authored or a separately published UI specification.

The system is nearly flat: a single soft card shadow (`rgba(0,0,0,0.08) 0px 4px 8px`) lifts white news cards, and almost everything else separates through flat color bands and thin `#dddddd` hairlines on a light `#f6f6f6` surface. Interactive chrome leans on the 8px-radius rectangle — solid dark buttons and matching outline buttons (transparent with a 1px dark or white border), all at 52px height with 14px 20px padding. Cards round more generously at 12-20px. The neutral text ladder steps from black through `#303030`, `#5e5e5e`, the muted nav grey `#777777`, down to a faint `#b4b4b4`. Reading that restraint as what distinguishes Greencar from heavier automotive and rental sites, and reading the result as a clean, modern, mobility-forward aesthetic, is a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; it is not Greencar-authored or a separately published UI specification.

The source's §11 narrative places the company as one of Korea's pioneering car-sharing services, established in **2011** as the country began adopting on-demand short-term vehicle rental. In **2015** it became a subsidiary of **Lotte Rental (롯데렌탈)**, Korea's largest car-rental group, and today positions itself — in its own words on the homepage — as a `프리미엄 카셰어링 서비스` operated `롯데렌탈과 함께`. The brand's stated promise is to go `이동을 넘어 사람과 공간을 연결하는` — beyond movement, connecting people and space — designing `사용자 중심의 새로운 이동 경험`. The product matured from round-trip car-sharing into a full mobility line-up: G car (round-trip and one-way car-sharing, G car zone, G car Pass, G car M, G car Biz), Movus, and Clingwash car-care. The corporate brand frames this expansion as `고객 중심의 모빌리티 풀 라인업` rather than a fleet catalog. Official history and the live homepage meta provide that narrative context; they do not by themselves supply interface tokens. What the source says Greencar refuses, visible in its design: the heavy, discount-driven chrome of legacy rental sites (no aggressive deal banners, no cluttered price tables on the brand surface) and the cold institutional palette of automotive incumbents. What it embraces: a calm editorial corporate surface, a single confident brand green, the aspirational latin voice of Outfit over the functional Korean clarity of Pretendard, and a near-flat layout that lets the green identity and the human copy carry the page. Reading that refuse/embrace pair as a design stance is a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; it is not Greencar-authored or a separately published UI specification.

### Principles

These 5 items are a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; they are not Greencar-authored or a separately published UI specification.

1. **Green is identity, dark is action.** *UI implication:* reserve the brand green (`#00c88c`) for hero panels and accent links; keep buttons in neutral dark ink (`#222222`) so the next step is unambiguous.
2. **Mobility as a human experience.** Greencar frames car-sharing as connecting people and space. *UI implication:* lead with aspirational, inclusive copy; avoid transactional, price-first layouts on the brand surface.
3. **Two fonts, two voices.** *UI implication:* Outfit carries the aspirational latin voice; Pretendard carries the functional Korean reading layer — never blur the two.
4. **Flat and calm.** *UI implication:* separate with color bands and hairlines, not stacked shadows; keep the page editorial and quick to scan.
5. **One brand color.** *UI implication:* green is the only saturated hue — don't introduce a competing accent that would dilute the identity signal.
