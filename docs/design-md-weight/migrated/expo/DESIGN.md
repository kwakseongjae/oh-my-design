This contract covers three first-party public marketing pages inspected on 2026-07-13 — the marketing home at `https://expo.dev/`, the marketing services page at `https://expo.dev/services`, and the marketing pricing page at `https://expo.dev/pricing`. The public home page gives that story a product shape: a React Native framework plus cloud services for building, deploying, updating, and observing applications. Expo’s 2026 company story also extends from a framework foundation toward AI-native universal-app development and Expo Agent, and that current evolution is explicit in Expo’s April 2026 first-party funding post: the company raised a $45 million Series B and introduced Expo Agent while explaining a broader effort to make app creation easier and faster. ### Principles

These four items, including their *UI implication* notes, are a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; they are not Expo-authored or a separately published UI specification. The numbered stems paraphrase Expo’s own public product message, brand manifesto, and EAS documentation; the implications underneath them are this reconstruction’s reading of what that means for an interface.

1. **Universal application delivery.** Expo’s public product message spans Android, iOS, and web. *UI implication:* explain cross-platform capability with product evidence, not platform-specific visual invention.
2. **Neutral, reliable foundations.** The official brand manifesto explicitly contrasts Expo’s foundation with more expressive platform design languages. *UI implication:* prefer the observed quiet light hierarchy over decorative color.
3. **Simple, spacious, consistent presentation.** These are first-party brand-guideline goals. *UI implication:* retain measured spacing and rounded-action roles; do not manufacture a full grid.
4. **Concrete developer operations.** EAS documentation describes build, submit, update, workflow, hosting, and observability services. *UI implication:* use concise, task-oriented labels rather than generic benefit copy.

The five application rules below are a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; they are not Expo-authored or a separately published UI specification. Each one restates a boundary of the supplied evidence rather than a published Expo rule.

- Use the observed light canvas, white surface, near-black text, and black conversion-action hierarchy together.
- Keep header, hero, and compact pricing actions in their separately measured 36px, 48px, and 32px geometries.
- Use Inter for general marketing UI and reserve JetBrains Mono for evidence-backed code-oriented text.
- Preserve the close black hover and pressed values only for the compact pricing action where they were captured.
- Use the 8px dialog panel and its measured shadow only for a pricing-dialog-like overlay.

### Avoid

The five prohibitions below are a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; they are not Expo-authored or a separately published UI specification. Each one restates a boundary of the supplied evidence rather than a published Expo rule.

- Do not turn a locally observed green or blue value into a global Expo semantic token without element-level evidence.
- Do not substitute `ui-monospace` for JetBrains Mono; its two computed uses lacked a loaded FontFace match.
- Do not infer inputs, cards, authenticated dashboard controls, native app components, or responsive breakpoints from this marketing-only bundle.
- Do not treat the official brand asset page or documentation chrome as a source for these marketing component measurements.
- Do not add error, success, input, card, or mobile rules from this reference.

### Font evidence

The five-way split below — live marketing surface-use, live code-oriented surface-use, official font/license context, declared-only, and unresolved — sorts the font evidence into classes. That sorting, the reading of those classes as domains that may not fill one another, and each item’s promotion decision (Inter as the UI family, JetBrains Mono as the mono family rather than general UI type, `ui-monospace` as neither) are a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; they are not Expo-authored or a separately published UI specification. The use counts, the loaded sources, and the license statements inside the items are verified.

- **Live marketing surface-use:** Inter was both computed and loaded for 990 visible uses from first-party `static.expo.dev` WOFF2 sources. It is the UI family for this reference.
- **Live code-oriented surface-use:** JetBrains Mono was both computed and loaded for six visible uses from first-party WOFF2 sources. It is retained as the mono family, not as general UI type.
- **Official font/license context:** Inter’s author distributes it under SIL OFL 1.1; JetBrains Mono is likewise OFL 1.1 and available for commercial and non-commercial use. These licenses describe the fonts, not an Expo-exclusive asset.
- **Declared-only:** none in the supplied bundle.
- **Unresolved:** `ui-monospace` appeared twice without a matching loaded FontFace; it is not a token or substitute.

### Family

- **UI family:** Inter — canonical for this scope because computed visible use and loaded first-party WOFF2 sources agree.
- **Mono family:** JetBrains Mono — retained for the six observed code-oriented uses only, not as general UI type.
- No system or fallback stack is presented as the Expo face, and `ui-monospace` is not substituted for JetBrains Mono.

Making agreement between computed visible use and loaded first-party sources the condition for promoting a family, and restricting JetBrains Mono to the code-oriented role it was observed in, are a derived editorial implementation inference from the verified surfaces; they are not Expo-authored or a separately published UI specification.
