## What the images suggest

### Onboarding

Raw kept the reversibility promise in a dedicated callout and used a filled
selected-role treatment. OmD converted the reassurance into ordinary body copy
and weakened the selected state to an outline plus a small dot. Both fulfilled
the task, but the blind judgment consistently preferred the version with the
stronger decision cue and risk-reduction hierarchy.

### Locale handoff

Raw retained a visible purple active-tab outline and stronger brand-accent
hierarchy. OmD made the tab row and section marker substantially more neutral.
The blind judgment consistently preferred Raw on every axis. This is evidence
against treating restraint, token compliance, or component cleanup as
permission to desaturate a useful state distinction.

### Incident operations

Raw separated the incident queue, response notes, and next safe action into
clear spatial regions. OmD consolidated them into one large bordered console.
The primary pass preferred Raw for Functionality, Usability, and Ship
Preference, while preferring OmD for Fidelity. The reversed pass selected the
opposite candidate on all four axes, so this example is diagnostic of
ambiguity or side sensitivity rather than a stable winner.

## Bounded conclusion

The accepted deterministic closures protect behavior, contrast, geometry
roles, focusability, and unknown facts. They do not yet protect **visual
equity**: an existing high-value hierarchy, reassurance cue, active-state
signal, or task-oriented section boundary can be flattened while every
objective gate remains green.

The next patch should not encode “Raw wins,” preserve every old visual choice,
or copy these three layouts. It should require the agent to identify the
small number of existing visual decisions that materially help the user make
the task decision, and require explicit authority before weakening or replacing
them. The hypothesis must be tested on fresh current-version artifacts.
